F4 — Wrong-attester fixture violated local initialization: FIXED.
  Policies and their own assignments remain unchanged and legal. Only the
  RPE1 invocation of the shared attestation checker targets E2 using E2's
  joined entry, while acting as P1. The query observes CertIssued(P1,E2,...).
  This tests the attester guard through a verifier/service-target fixture;
  it is not a claim that normal RPE1 code spontaneously changes its target.

F5 — Pre-policy recipient labels treated as authenticated parties: FIXED.
  PolicySent now records the actual recipient public key, and the auxiliary
  PolicyAcceptedKeys query links acceptance to a send to that same key.
  Receiver-key binding is inside the authenticated payload. The outer party
  labels remain attacker-controlled. EvidenceChecked is correctly documented
  as a provisional collection-slot check; final party-key association comes
  from signature verification, Quote-policy hash equality, and policy Self.

Retained protocol structure

Each primary role gathers all distinct required Quote slots before publishing
policies, checks every received policy against its authenticated endpoint,
Quote commitment and Self, and aggregates its own and all required accepted
inputs in canonical party order. FullJoin follows that actual computation.
Every required authenticated matching confirmation precedes Confirm. The
two-party service is invoked only after Confirm. All traffic is public and
attacker-scheduled. No private delivery channel, session field, added round,
member-set hash, peer owner-signature check, or pre-distributed global policy
list was introduced.

QuoteReceived, QuoteSignatureChecked and QuoteVerified are observation events
added respectively after packet receipt, hardware-signature verification, and
instance-key/challenge binding verification. They add no wire message or guard
and permit negative tests to demonstrate the exact point reached before a
later check rejects. In particular, QuoteVerified is deliberately BEFORE
policy appraisal; its name must not be interpreted as QuoteSatisfied.

The no-confirm primary diff removes only the confirmation transmission,
reception, waiting and equality checks. The same FullJoin, comparable Confirm
event, policy inputs and primary queries remain. Its successful queries can
be interpreted only under the same bounded configuration as the primary model.

Finite-domain join specialization

The final process uses a finite specialization of joinstar on the exact
canonical input domain: P1 has the fixed honest constraints; in N=3 P2 has
the same constraints; the final policy is one of the 11 catalog profiles.
The RPE still actually receives and authenticates those policies before
calling the join. The specialized result contains normalized constraints,
not raw policy history or a participant-input encoding.

The emitted specialization was independently compared in ProVerif against
generic jointcb/joinentry semantics for 12 cases at N=2 and 12 at N=3: all
11 catalog inputs plus the honest base. Success, failure and result equality
are checked; the checker reads the emitted tables rather than recomputing
expected outputs with the Python merge implementation. The completed log
reports both WrongSpecialization events unreachable. This supports exactness
on that documented domain, not for arbitrary policies or arbitrary operands.

Coordination diagnostic interpretation

D1 at N=2 checks CertIssued(P1,E1,...,x,...) ==> FullJoin(P2,x).
D1 at N=3 checks Confirm(P1,x) ==> FullJoin(P2,x) AND FullJoin(P3,x).
These are optional diagnostic correspondences, not replacements for Q1-Q4.
The full-protocol result establishes that these peer computations precede the
observed local service/completion event. It does not establish that peers have
already received all their confirmations or completed initialization.

Both no-confirm false results include 'A trace has been found.' The recorded
trace delivers genuine policies to P1 while delaying policy delivery to other
RPEs. P1 locally joins and completes; at N=2 it also issues a policy-compliant
certificate. The required peer FullJoin event has not occurred. This is loss
of startup coordination, not a differing-policy or invalid-certificate attack.
The retained Q1-Q4 queries still hold in these no-confirm models.

The reachability results are existential execution witnesses, not liveness
guarantees against message withholding. They must be classified separately
from a failed safety query. The final run package must retain the matching
source hashes, full output, time and exact query for each result.

Remaining scope and limitations

1. One static initialization with exactly N protected RPE role instances is
   modeled. The required roster is a fixed common configuration, not inferred
   from received messages. Arbitrary additional compliant RPEs, independent
   inconsistent configurations, dynamic membership and multiple overlapping
   initialization runs are outside these claims.
2. The corrupted owner chooses one of 11 documented profiles. These exercise
   compatible, weaker, stronger and conflicting constraints but do not cover
   every combination of legal policy components. Exact identity atoms are
   symbolic; SVN and TCB requirement/status sets are explicitly bounded.
3. Stage 1 authorization is assumed to have completed correctly. The result
   does not prove owner signatures, policy provisioning or startup designation
   authenticity. Local public-key de-duplication explicitly models distinct
   peers; this source review does not verify its presence in implementation.
4. The authenticated exchange is represented by domain-separated signatures
   bound to recipient keys on a public channel. It assumes perfect primitives;
   it does not prove TLS/channel establishment or policy confidentiality.
5. Positive RPE appraisal in the primary model uses compliant RPE code and
   Good platform status; rejection of bad code/status is examined in fixtures.
6. Stage 3 proves the represented identity/TCB/attester and key/challenge/
   policy bindings. Quote parsing, collateral chains/revocation infrastructure,
   real TEE isolation, certificate consumption and TLS behavior remain abstract
   or unmodeled as explained by the dedicated Stage 3 audit.
7. Fixed-input no-confirm success does not establish that confirmation is
   unnecessary in every deployment. The new D1 diagnostic concretely shows
   that startup coordination is weaker without it, while the checked policy
   agreement and issuance-compliance properties remain true. D1 concerns P1
   at each modeled size, not an arbitrary-N global completion barrier.
   The old incomplete-input/no-confirm attack remains historical evidence only.
8. The 24 regression fixtures do not exhaust all malformed packet/configuration
   combinations. There is no separate wrong-instance-public-key fixture;
   key/challenge association is nevertheless represented by the hchal check
   and the auxiliary issuance-to-ChallengeSent/QuoteProduced correspondence.
